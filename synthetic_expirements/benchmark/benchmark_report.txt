── 1. Generation Rationale ────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  This benchmark framework was designed to provide a fully controlled,
  multi-configuration experimental environment for the methodological
  validation of a hybrid quantum-classical biomarker selection pipeline
  applied to breast cancer genomics.

  The core motivation is epistemic: because real clinical datasets have unknown
  ground truth regarding which genes are causal versus merely associated with
  disease, it is impossible to objectively measure feature selection recall or
  precision from real data alone. A suite of synthetic datasets with deliberately
  implanted signal structures enables exact quantitative evaluation of:

    • Feature selection recall (fraction of implanted biomarkers recovered)
    • False-positive rate (noise genes selected in top-K)
    • Algorithmic sensitivity to noise, sparsity, imbalance, and dimensionality
    • QUBO/QAOA convergence across problem regimes
    • FP-Growth itemset recovery under varying support levels

── 2. Modelling Assumptions ───────────────────────────────────────────────────

  (a) Binary mutation encoding: x_{ij} ∈ {0,1} mirrors VCF binarisation in
      real tumour sequencing pipelines.

  (b) Independence of background genes: noise genes are assigned independent
      Bernoulli rates from U(rate_min, rate_max), approximating the somatic
      mutation background in breast cancer WES data.

  (c) Class-conditional mutation rates: cancer driver genes use TCGA BRCA
      calibrated rates (Ciriello et al. 2015; CGAL 2012), scaled by the
      biomarker_signal_scale parameter to simulate weak vs strong signal regimes.

  (d) Co-occurrence as joint Bernoulli: co-mutation patterns are implanted by
      forcing joint activation, sufficient for FP-Growth itemset validation.

  (e) Tumour heterogeneity: a configurable fraction of cancer patients exhibits
      attenuated mutation rates (configurable range), approximating molecular
      subtype diversity in breast cancer.

  (f) Correlated noise blocks: groups of genes are made internally correlated
      via a Bernoulli-copying rule, simulating CNV co-amplification blocks.

── 3. Experiment Regime Overview ──────────────────────────────────────────────

  baseline          Moderate noise, strong signal, balanced 55/45 split.
                    Performance ceiling reference.

  high_noise        Background rates 5–12%, 20 correlated blocks (ρ=0.85).
                    Robustness against noisy genomic background.

  extreme_sparsity  Background rates 0.1–0.8%, co-mutation support 15%.
                    Realistic low-TMB WES landscape.

  class_imbalance   20% cancer fraction. Rare-disease screening scenario.

  weak_signal       Biomarker rates scaled to 40% of TCGA baseline.
                    Detection limit characterisation.

  high_dimensional  10 000 genes × 1 500 patients. Justifies MI/LASSO
                    pre-filter before QUBO/QAOA (limited by qubit count).

  heterogeneity     40% subtype B fraction, 30–60% attenuation.
                    Molecular subtype diversity simulation.

  correlated_noise  30 blocks × 25 genes, ρ=0.95. Adversarial confounding
                    to exploit MI marginal scoring weakness.

── 4. Limitations ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  • No real tumour clonal dynamics (branching evolution, subclonal architecture).
  • Patient-level TMB variability not modelled; rates are gene-specific only.
  • Co-mutation patterns modelled as exact joint activation.
  • Binary encoding discards VAF, zygosity, and functional consequence.
  • No survival, treatment, or imaging data; classification task only.
  • Calibrated on TCGA BRCA aggregates; subtype/stage-specific landscapes
    are not distinguished.

── 5. Intended Use ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  This framework is intended EXCLUSIVELY for:
    (1) Methodological validation and algorithm benchmarking.
    (2) Ablation studies across statistical regimes.
    (3) Proof-of-concept demonstration of the MI + LASSO + QUBO/QAOA pipeline.
    (4) FP-Growth co-mutation discovery validation.
    (5) QAOA/QUBO convergence characterisation.

  It is NOT intended for:
    • Clinical decision support or diagnosis.
    • Biological hypothesis generation about real cancer drivers.
    • Training production ML models for medical use.
    • Publication of results as biological findings.

── How This Multi-Experiment Framework Strengthens Methodological Validation ──

  1. GROUND-TRUTH EVALUATION
     Since biomarkers and co-mutation patterns are deliberately implanted,
     you can compute exact Recall@K, Precision@K, and F1 for feature
     selection across all 8 experiment regimes — impossible with real data.

  2. ABLATION STUDIES VIA CONTROLLED REGIMES
     Each experiment isolates one statistical factor (noise, sparsity, imbalance,
     signal strength, dimensionality, heterogeneity). Cross-experiment comparison
     isolates the effect of each factor independently.

  3. MULTI-SEED VARIANCE ESTIMATION
     Running each experiment with seeds {42, 123, 999} allows you to report
     mean ± std for all metrics (Recall@K, AUC, QUBO solution quality),
     demonstrating statistical robustness rather than cherry-picked results.

  4. HIGH-DIMENSIONAL JUSTIFICATION (high_dimensional regime)
     The 10 000-gene dataset directly motivates the MI+LASSO pre-filter stage:
     QUBO/QAOA encoding of 10 000 binary variables requires ~10 000 qubits,
     exceeding all near-term hardware. Runtime tables across regimes demonstrate
     why dimensionality reduction is non-optional, not merely convenient.

  5. CORRELATED NOISE ADVERSARIAL TEST (correlated_noise regime)
     The adversarial correlated block structure specifically exploits the
     weakness of marginal MI scoring (high correlation inflates MI of
     non-causal genes). LASSO and QUBO joint-sparsity objectives are
     expected to outperform marginal MI here — a testable hypothesis.

  6. QUBO/QAOA CONVERGENCE ANALYSIS
     With known ground truth, you can plot biomarker recovery vs. QAOA circuit
     depth (p layers) and compare quantum annealing behaviour to classical
     greedy baselines across all 8 regimes — producing convergence curves
     expected in a quantum computing thesis chapter.

── What Claims MUST NOT Be Made from Synthetic Experiments ───────────────────

  ✗  "TP53 is a real breast cancer biomarker in our patients."
  ✗  "QAOA outperforms classical optimisation for breast cancer diagnosis."
  ✗  "The co-mutation of PIK3CA and BRCA1 is a novel biological discovery."
  ✗  "Our model achieves 92% accuracy on breast cancer mutation data."

  ✓  SAFE claims:
     "On the baseline synthetic benchmark (seed=42), MI+LASSO recovers X%
      of implanted biomarkers at K=50 features."
     "Across 3 seeds, mean Recall@50 ± std in the high_noise regime is
      Y ± Z%, compared to W ± V% in the baseline."
     "QAOA with p=3 layers achieves Q% of the optimal QUBO solution quality
      on the baseline feature selection instance."
     "FP-Growth recovers all 6 implanted co-mutation patterns at minimum
      support threshold 0.20 in the baseline, but only 3/6 in extreme_sparsity."
